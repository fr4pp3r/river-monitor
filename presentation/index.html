03 · STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM

Statement of the problem

Flooding is a persistent threat in Brgy. Elvita, Narra, Palawan — a flood-vulnerable barangay along a major river system with limited structural flood-mitigation works, unreliable internet connectivity, and continued reliance on manual observation and threshold-based warnings. Existing centralized systems can be unreachable during network outages, and few documented implementations combine offline, edge-based hybrid AI analytics for rural barangay deployment. FLAIRE addresses this gap.

1

What are the key problems and limitations in current flood-warning practices and data utilization in Brgy. Elvita, Narra, Palawan, and how can these be addressed through an offline-first, AI-based flood early-warning system?

2

How can a Fuzzy-AI riverine flood-risk classification system be designed and developed for Brgy. Elvita that integrates cached seven-day weather and tide forecast data from OpenMeteo (updated at least daily, every six hours, and stored in a local database); real-time water-level and rainfall data from a LoRa-based hydrological sensor node transmitted to a Raspberry Pi 5 edge device; a hybrid AI pipeline in which a Random Forest model estimates flood propensity using R1, R3, R7, rain_days, TMAX, TMIN, TideMax, and TideMin while a fuzzy-logic classifier with 20 rules classifies flood risk as Receding, Alert, Alarm, or Critical; and a local web dashboard accessible through a LAN, enabling BDRRMC personnel to monitor real-time sensor data, AI-based risk classifications, and visualizations without internet connectivity, with alerts delivered through the dashboard and SMS?

3

How consistently does the Random Forest flood-propensity model reproduce the rule-derived Low, Medium, and High flood-propensity categories on a held-out test set, in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score?

4

How did the FLAIRE prototype perform during a limited field deployment in terms of sensor-data capture, data logging, and data quality?

5

How acceptable is the developed FLAIRE system to the BDRRMC focal persons of Brgy. Elvita, Narra, Palawan in terms of functionality, usability, reliability, perceived contribution to disaster preparedness, attitude toward use, and intention to use, as measured through a Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)-based interview or survey?

04 · Significance of the study

Significance of the study

Who benefits

BDRRMC (Barangay Elvita)

Offline-first, AI-enhanced flood early-warning: flood-risk classifications from real-time sensor data and cached forecasts through a LAN dashboard — supporting informed decisions, preemptive evacuation planning, and resource allocation even during internet or power interruptions.

Barangay officials & residents

Timely flood notifications through SMS alerts and dashboard-based monitoring, improving community preparedness and resilience in a flood-prone, low-connectivity setting.

Academic community

A documented, reproducible offline-first hybrid Random Forest + fuzzy-logic flood-risk classification for a rural Philippine barangay, with an honest account of its labeling and validation limits.

Policy-makers & future deployments

Evidence that barangay-scale early warning can run with minimal internet dependency and edge-based AI — an initial basis for scaling low-cost, power-resilient monitoring to similar communities.

05 · Scope and Delimitation

Scope and Delimitation

In scope vs. boundaries

In scope

  • Design, development, and pilot deployment of FLAIRE — offline-first hybrid Fuzzy-AI riverine flood-risk classification and early-warning system, Brgy. Elvita, Narra, Palawan (one vulnerable river section)
  • Historical data (PAGASA, NAMRIA) + cached Open-Meteo forecasts (6 h refresh), stored locally in SQLite
  • LoRa sensor node → Raspberry Pi 5 edge device; RF flood propensity (8 features) + 20-rule fuzzy logic → four risk levels
  • LAN dashboard + SMS alerts; BDRRMC retains alert authority
  • Held-out test-set evaluation (80/20) and TAM acceptability survey (n = 2 focal persons)

Delimitations

  • Riverine flooding only in Brgy. Elvita — excludes coastal flooding, upstream flash floods, areas outside the barangay
  • Single sensor node; no full river-system coverage
  • Decision support, not prediction: rule-derived labels; no measured forecast lead time; no threshold-baseline comparison
  • Preliminary four-event check only; limited deployment duration (~21.5 h)
  • n = 2 survey — preliminary, not generalizable
  • Dependent on historical / forecast / real-time data availability and quality

10 · Summary, conclusion and recommendation

Summary, conclusion & recommendation

Chapter 5 — what the study establishes

Summary — key findings

  • MDRRMO alerts are municipality-wide; monitoring gaps remain — current practice is manual (rain gauge, visual observation, tide calendar).
  • RF reproduced its rule-derived labels (94.26% accuracy) — consistency, not independent flood-prediction skill.
  • Pilot logged 13,568 records over ~21.5 h at Receding; battery depletion ended deployment; acceptability 5.00 / 4.75 (n = 2).

Conclusion

  • Feasibility established — the offline-first hybrid RF + fuzzy-logic prototype runs end-to-end on a Pi 5.
  • Decision-support, not prediction — the BDRRMC makes the alert call.
  • Human-in-the-loop alerting (SMS at Alarm / Critical, BDRRMC confirmation) is essential.

Recommendation

  • Use independent, BDRRMC-validated flood labels.
  • Validate chronologically (e.g., train 2020–2023, test 2024).
  • Harden power: panel placement, battery capacity, voltage monitoring.
  • Expand the acceptability sample beyond n = 2.
